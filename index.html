Contests you play — flat payout profile

You currently enter Solo Shot and Four Seamer only. Both share the same payout shape: 10% of the prize pool to first and roughly a 26% cash rate. That is a flat tournament, not a top-heavy one, and every decision below is calibrated to it.

  • Stokastic "percent to first" = 10%. Not 25%, not 40%. Chris's 25% figure is for flagship large-field 20-max contests, which you are not playing yet.
  • Pool size = 2,000 (your tier max). Set it as close to the real field as your plan allows.
  • Floor matters more than you would think. Paying a quarter of the field rewards reliably-good lineups over occasionally-spectacular ones. Lean closer to your cash instincts than to lottery-ticket instincts.
  • Deep contrarian is near-worthless here. Nailing an 8%-owned stack that nobody has only pays off if it carries you to first — and first is only 10% of the pool.

When you expand into flagship / top-heavy contests, this whole tab needs a second set of bands. Do not reuse these numbers there.

The four approaches — tag every import

Any single approach looks broken over a short window. Chris runs 15+ approaches at ~200 lineups each nightly and grades them — one night an approach takes first, the next night the same one tanks. You cannot run that volume, but you can tag which method built each set so a cold stretch is distinguishable from a broken method. Pick one approach per slate, tag the import, and let the dashboard's GPP by approach card accumulate.

ApproachHow to build itBetting on
Banded Rank teams by Vegas implied total, cut into chalk / middle / deep bands, pick teams inside each band by SIM ROI, allocate to the target percentages in the band table above. Vegas rank being a decent proxy for field ownership, and the middle band (ranks 3-7) producing the plurality of winners.
Flat spread Roughly equal exposure across every team with a viable stack. On a 4-gamer that is ~8 teams; take a 5-man stack from each with the best hitters. Little or no ranking. SIM ROI top-N Take Chris's top-N stacks by SIM ROI and build the book straight off that list, ignoring Vegas rank entirely. Weight by the ROI figures, using the joint-ROI arm pairings. The ownership-adjusted money grade already being the right answer, with no further filtering needed.
Manual Hand-built off your own read of the slate — weather, park, a specific matchup you like. Also the tag for anything that does not fit the other three. Slate-specific judgment the models are not capturing.

Read the results honestly. At 10-20 GPP entries a slate you accumulate a few hundred lineups per approach in a month. That is enough to catch a clearly broken approach and nowhere near enough to pick a winner between two decent ones. Chris sees approaches trade places at 200 lineups per night. Treat the breakdown as a smoke alarm, not a leaderboard, and give it real time before acting.

One approach per slate. Splitting a slate dilutes both samples and makes the comparison slower to resolve. Rotate between slates instead.

Uniques — set before favoriting

Uniques is a minimum-difference constraint: 5 uniques means any two lineups must differ by at least 5 players. With 10 roster spots that caps overlap at half the roster.

  • Why it matters: an unconstrained optimizer produces near-duplicates. The top 150 by sim ROI are mostly permutations of the same few builds — swap a corner infielder, swap a min-priced outfielder. Without uniques you can enter 150 lineups and hold about six real positions.
  • Order matters. Set uniques first, then favorite your top N. Favoriting first and constraining after just filters an already-converged set. Chris sets 5 uniques before favoriting his top 150.
  • Scale it to book size. Roughly 2-3 uniques on a 20-lineup book, 3-4 on 50, 5 on 150+. High uniques on a small book forces you off your best lineups by the eighth or so entry.
  • Go low on 4-gamers. With 8 teams the structurally distinct space is small, and a high setting manufactures artificial differences rather than meaningful ones.
  • Uniques is a cleaner lever than team caps for genuine variety. A cap says "no more than X% on MIA" but leaves those lineups otherwise identical; uniques forces real structural difference. If a concentrated book also ran low uniques, it is even less diversified than the exposure numbers suggest.
Band workflow — do this every slate
  1. List all teams by Vegas implied total, highest to lowest. This is your rank order, and it approximates what the field is looking at.
  2. Cut the bands per the table below, based on slate size.
  3. Inside each band, rank by Chris's SIM ROI and take the number of teams listed. If no SplashPlay data that slate, fall back to Stokastic Stack Score.
  4. Assign exposure to hit the target percentage for each band across your book.
  5. Assign arms by joint ROI — see the next card. On small slates this is your main differentiator.

On a 20-lineup book, standard slate: ~7-8 lineups chalk, ~10-11 middle, ~1-2 deep. On a 4-game slate cut the book to 10-12 entries — edge compresses when the pool is thin, and caps barely bind with only 8 teams to rotate through.

Why chalk is not zero: a quarter of winning stacks come from top-2 Vegas teams. Running 0% chalk was the single biggest structural problem in the worst GPP stretch. Chalk should be floored at roughly half the field's win rate, but never eliminated.

Small-slate note: "ranks 8+" does not exist on a 4-gamer. There is nowhere to hide, so chalk goes up and differentiation has to come from bat combinations inside stacks and from pitcher-stack pairings — not from team selection, since everyone can see all 8 implied totals.

Reading Chris's top-stacks table
  • $ % is your column, not W %. Solo Shot and Four Seamer pay 26% of the field. A stack with strong cash rate and mediocre win rate is a good stack for these contests. Chasing the W column pulls you back toward boom-or-bust, which is the same error as the old 40%-to-first setting arriving through a different door.
  • Joint ROI is the small-slate lever. "⚡ +63.8% JOINT ROI / SP Eovaldi, Tolle" means the stack paired with those arms tested far better than the stack alone. On an 8-team slate where team choice barely differentiates, this is where the real combinatorics live. Use it to assign arms across the book.
  • Treat the numbers as tiers, not a strict order. 260 lineups is a small sample and thin ones are flagged ⚠. +11.3% and +8% are not meaningfully different — sort into clearly-positive / neutral / clearly-negative and break ties on Vegas rank so you stay inside your bands.
  • Match stack size to the table you are reading. 3-man ROI figures are not comparable to 5-man — smaller stacks post better ROI partly because they are cheaper to be wrong about. Use the 3-man table for secondary/mini stacks, the 5-man table for your primary.
  • LEV and 🎯 are context, not rankers. LEV is sim win contribution vs field ownership; 🎯 is market-implied explosion probability. Useful for understanding why a stack grades well. SIM ROI already accounts for both.
What the data says (SplashPlay shark analysis)
  • Arms: accept consensus. Winners roster chalk pitchers and differentiate with bats. Never fade the top arm for ownership reasons. Choosing among co-equal top arms by leverage is fine — that is not a fade.
  • Winning stack Vegas rank: p25 = 2, p50 = 5, p75 = 7. The meat of the distribution is ranks 3-7, but a quarter of winners come from the top 2. Do not zero out chalk.
  • Implied totals 4.3-4.8 are the eruption band — the range winning stacks come from most often.
  • 5-man stacks are the majority. The extra hitter matters for ceiling.
  • Sub-10%-owned bats inside mid-board stacks are the cleanest source of leverage plus ceiling at once.
  • Bring-backs appear in only 21-29% of winners — not structural. The 6th spot goes to best available anywhere on the slate.
  • Spend near max. ~$49.8k on clean chalk slates, ~$48.5k on messy ones. Raw points win contests.
Stokastic settings for these contests
  • Pool size = 2,000 (tier max). This is the simulated field size — there is no separate field setting on your interface.
  • Percent to first = 10%. Verified from the actual payout tables for both Solo Shot and Four Seamer.
  • Exposure caps live in the Pre-Contest Simulator, not the Contest Generator. After the sim: set caps → Adjust Favorites → Export Favorites. Adjust Favorites is the apply step after any tweak.
  • Team stack caps bind primary stacks only, not secondary. Real chalk exposure runs higher than the cap suggests. Setting chalk primary to your target still works cleanly, since secondary does not consume it.
  • Point the team cap at the chalk team to suppress it rather than capping your leverage plays. Let top-2 leverage teams run 30-35%.
  • Projection edits happen before the sim and flow through correlated players. ROI boosts happen after and only re-rank lineups. Boost-then-cap forces an exact exposure.
  • Leave projected ownership alone. That field models the field's ownership, not your preference. It is the easiest place to fool yourself, and Stokastic's estimates are accurate.
  • Verify shape, not just caps. Caps control exposure, not construction. Count true 5-man stacks in the export before you upload.

Slate size

Small (1-4 games)

  • Still default to 5-man stacks — raw points win contests, and the ceiling matters more than the constraint costs.
  • Chalk band goes up to ~40%. There is nowhere to hide with 8 teams and the field sees the same totals.
  • Differentiate via bat combinations inside stacks and pitcher-stack pairings (joint ROI), not team selection.
  • Cut the book to 10-12 entries. Edge compresses when the pool is thin.
  • Down to 1 reliable game → skip GPP, cash only or sit out.

Large (10+ games)

  • Full band split available: chalk 35-40%, middle 50-55%, deep 5-10%.
  • Ownership spreads thin — a real deep band exists, unlike on small slates.
  • Run the full book. Exposure caps actually bind at this many teams.

Satellites (Winner-Take-All)
Stokastic setting to use

In the Sims, after generating lineups sort by Win % (or Top 1% finish rate) instead of Sim ROI. Sim ROI optimizes across all contest types. Win % specifically identifies lineups most likely to finish first — exactly what satellites require. Use pool size 1,000, then take the top lineup by Win %.

Build principles
  • Maximum ceiling, full correlation. 5-man stack from the highest over/under game, consecutive batting order. Bring-back 1-2 from opposing team. SP from a different game facing a weak offense.
  • Spend up everywhere. Do not balance salary around mid-tier players. Top-projected players at every slot even if it forces a minimum-salary fill elsewhere.
  • One game environment, all in. You are betting that one game explodes. Do not hedge across multiple games — that is GPP thinking, not satellite thinking.
  • If you can see opponents after lock — target a different game than the majority. Winning with the same stack as 10 of 14 opponents is random. Being on a different game that goes off independently wins outright.
What to avoid
  • Do not play a cash lineup — consistent floor finishes 2nd or 3rd repeatedly and wins nothing.
  • Do not worry about ownership — 14 opponents is too small for ownership leverage to matter.
  • Do not spread across multiple game environments — diversification kills ceiling in winner-take-all.
  • Small slate satellite: field convergence is extreme with few stacking options. Lower budget or skip — edge is minimal.
Showdown GPP & Cash
Format basics

DK — 6 players

  • 1 Captain (1.5x pts, 1.5x salary) + 5 FLEX
  • Must have at least 1 player from each team
  • Captain choice determines most of your ceiling

FD — 5 players

  • 1 MVP (2x pts) + 4 FLEX
  • Must have at least 1 player from each team
  • MVP slot has even more weight than DK Captain
Cash
  • Captain/MVP = highest projected player in the game. Usually top hitter or ace SP. Stokastic projection sort, pick #1 at that slot.
  • Fill remaining spots with next-highest projected players regardless of team — don't force correlation in cash, just take floor.
  • Ensure at least 1 from each team (usually happens naturally with salary/projection math).
  • Don't put opposing hitters in the same lineup as your SP Captain — if he dominates, they score zero.
GPP — 3 lineup structures
1
Chalk Captain + correlation
  • Top projected player at Captain
  • 3-4 hitters from same team in FLEX (4-1 or 4-2 split)
  • High ownership but high floor — your safe GPP entry
2
Pivot Captain
  • 2nd-best projected player at Captain (lower owned)
  • Stack their team in FLEX
  • Wins outright if that team runs away with the game
3
Balanced / SP Captain
  • 3-3 split (DK) if both teams project well and over/under is high
  • OR: SP at Captain if Stokastic projects a dominant strikeout performance at low ownership (<15%)
  • Never stack SP Captain with opposing hitters

Stack structures: 4-1 or 5-1 = heavy lean on one team winning. 3-3 = high-scoring close game. 5-1 is highest ceiling, lowest probability — large field only.
